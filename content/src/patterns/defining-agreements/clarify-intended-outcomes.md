# Clarify Intended Outcomes

<summary>
Be explicit about the outcomes a significant decision or activity is intended to achieve, to build a shared understanding among those involved, and to evaluate the results of your actions.
</summary>

{{definition: The intended outcomes are the expected results of an activity, policy, action, project, or strategy.}}

Defining and agreeing on the outcomes you intend to achieve helps to build shared understanding about the purpose of a decision or activity, to avoid misconceptions, ensure coherent execution of agreements, and evaluate the results of your actions. 

Be sure to explain intended outcomes in a way that allows you to [define suitable metrics and monitor results effectively](section:define-and-monitor-metrics) to help determine whether or not they have been achieved and to identify opportunities for improvements based on what you learn. 

For simple activities, it is often sufficient to describe intended outcomes using the formulation: *Do X, so that* [*intended outcome*]. If more details are required, a good way of describing intended outcomes is with a list of criteria that need to be met. For tasks or projects, these are often referred to as “*acceptance criteria*.” They are tested and ticked off when the task or project is reviewed, which makes it simple to be specific about what kind of re-work may be required. Each of these criteria should be specific and measurable so that it is clear whether or not a criterion has been fulfilled.


## Ensure coherence

The **guidelines or imperatives** outlined in a policy should be coherent with the **outcomes** you want to achieve by implementing it. These outcomes must align with the [requirement](glossary:requirement) the policy is intended to fulfill, i.e., the intended outcomes should match the anticipated impact of fulfilling that requirement. 

While the **anticipated impact** overlaps with the intended outcomes of implementing a policy, each intended outcome refers to a *specific and measurable result*, whereas the anticipated impact can be broader. If implementing the policy is intended to fulfill the requirement entirely, then the sum of the intended outcome(s) should also result in the anticipated impact. In other cases, achieving one or more intended outcomes may only partially achieve the anticipated impact.

Finally, the policy and its outcomes must be appropriate for addressing the [driver](glossary:driver) that led to the requirement in the first place, ensuring coherence with both the driver and the anticipated impact.


## Examples

*See [Define and Monitor Metrics](section:define-and-monitor-metrics) for examples of metrics derived from the intended outcomes shown below.*

**Example 1:**

**Driver**: *We spend 25% of our work hours on admin work and this is leading to slow response time for customer requests and a growing number of complaints. We’re starting to develop a bad reputation and run the risk of losing customers and compromising future sales.*

**Requirement:** *We need to free up time for handling customer requirements in a timely manner so that we improve customer satisfaction and eliminate this type of complaint.*

**Policy*:** *Hire a specialist to handle some of the administrative work.*

**Intended outcome:** ***People working on the product will spend less time on administrative work, allowing them to focus more on the customer.***

**Example 2:**

**Driver**:* Information is unstructured, kept in silos, and sometimes unrecorded, leading to people working with missing or outdated information, which results in ineffectiveness and our clients’ needs being unmet.*

**Requirement**: *We need to store and share relevant information effectively to improve everyone’s ability to provide valuable solutions for our customers.*

**Policy:** *Schedule a meeting with representatives from the impacted domains to create an overview of the information each team has available, where it is stored, and what information the different teams need. Determine the next steps after that.*

**Intended outcomes:**

-   ***We have an overview of all the required information so that we can co-create a suitable structure for storing it.***
-   ***The above-mentioned overview enables teams to more easily find the information that is already available.***
-   ***We know what information is missing so that we can make a plan for how to provide it.***
